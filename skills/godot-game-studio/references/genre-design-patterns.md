# Genre Design Patterns: Strategy, Management, City-Builder, Colony Sim

Researched November 2026. Use as design prompts, not rules. Re-check recent releases when planning a 2027 launch.

## 1. Spatial, visible cause and effect

Recent breakout builders replace abstract resource bars with simulation the player can see and fix in space.

- Organic layout (Manor Lords style): roads are drawn freely, plots follow roads and terrain, villagers walk real paths, so a failure like starvation can be traced to a badly placed storehouse.
- Physical systems (Timberborn style): water flows, floods, and dries; crops fail when land dries; players reshape terrain with dams and channels; building also goes vertical.
- Design test: can the player diagnose the top 3 problems by looking at the world, without opening a panel? If not, add in-world signals (smoke, queues, empty carts, wilting crops, unhappy icons above homes).
- Grid builders are still valid (administrative / supply-chain puzzle framing); organic builders frame play as spatial reasoning. Pick one deliberately.

## 2. The late-game sprawl problem

Late-game sprawl turning into maintenance chores is the genre's hardest problem. Known answers:

- Remove a dimension (only build upward; limited land; one settlement).
- Close the resource loop so efficiency beats scale (things must fuel themselves).
- Let conditions move (shifting prices, seasons, events) so the "right" answer changes.
- Make expansion serve a goal beyond itself (fund expeditions, found a new outpost with new constraints, build a monument).
- Session-based structure (Against the Storm): each settlement is short-lived and ends before it overstays its welcome; meta-progression carries over.
- Caution: a very constrained game can feel "solved" in a few hours; pair constraints with variety.

## 3. Pressure without punishment (cozy-compatible)

- Pressure sources: seasons/winter, needs tiers, scarcity, disasters, raids, storms, reputation.
- Cozy variants: threats are scaled by difficulty mode, telegraphed early, and recoverable; failure costs time, not the save.
- Always telegraph: a timer, a forecast, a visible approaching event.
- Recovery path: on the default difficulty, the player can always climb out of a bad state.

## 4. Progression structures

- Population / needs tiers (Anno style): higher tiers need more goods and unlock new buildings; creates production chains and clear goals.
- Staged megaproject / monument: a long-term sink that shows progress in the world.
- Unlock trees tied to milestones rather than pure currency.
- Roguelite runs + meta-progression for replayability.
- Scenarios/maps with distinct constraints rather than one endless sandbox only.

## 5. Readability and onboarding

- First 5 minutes: one clear goal at a time, contextual hints, no tutorial wall.
- Every number shown should be actionable; hide the rest behind inspectors.
- Tooltips explain why something is locked or failing, not just what it is.
- Production chains are previewable before building.

## 6. Hook and presentation

- Cozy builders with strong visual identity (e.g. Tiny Glade's gridless "doodle" building with procedural detail) spread through short building clips; the act of building must look satisfying in a 5–10 second clip.
- Identity beats fidelity: a cohesive art style (diorama, papercraft, painterly) reads better than generic realism at indie budgets.
- Juice for building placement: snap feedback, small dust/pop, sound, procedural detail appearing.

## 7. Scope discipline for small teams

- Benchmark against comparable shipped games (team size, budget, content volume) before committing.
- Validate with a demo first, ideally shown at a Steam event, and learn what players like and dislike.
- Keep a ~25% contingency for delays.
- Weigh whether each feature adds enough value for its build time; shortcuts create later rework.

## Example reference games (study, don't copy)

Manor Lords (organic medieval building), Timberborn (water physics, vertical, 1.0 March 2026), Against the Storm (roguelite city builder, session structure), Going Medieval (colony sim, 1.0 March 2026), Frostpunk 2 (narrative/political pressure), Anno series (needs tiers, production chains), Kingdoms and Castles (small-scope medieval builder), Tiny Glade (cozy gridless diorama), Dorfromantik / Townscaper (cozy minimal builders).
